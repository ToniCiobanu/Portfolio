Are phones actually getting more expensive?

I took launch prices for notable phones (–) and converted them to dollars using the BLS Consumer Price Index. The main finding: once you compare like with like, the base iPhone has roughly tracked inflation. An earlier version of this project hid that by mixing two kinds of price.

The data

Phone prices: phones.csv

U.S. launch price of the base storage model. Each row keeps two prices: price_full (outright / no-contract) and price_contract (the two-year-contract headline price used 2008–2015). Every row has a source and a confidence level; anything marked "verify" is from contemporaneous press I haven't pinned to a primary source yet.

Inflation: cpi_u_annual.csv

BLS CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items, annual average (1982–84 = 100). 2025 = . Note: BLS did not collect October 2025 prices during the federal funding lapse, so the 2025 average is built from the months that were published.

Method


        
    
            
  • Headline numbers use price_full only. Contract prices are kept so the page can show what goes wrong if you use them.
    • 
            
  • Annual-average CPI for the launch year, not the launch month. Simple to explain, and using the launch month instead would move most results by a few percent at most.
    • 
            
  • Phones with no known full price (RAZR, BlackBerry 8700c, the US Galaxy S variants) appear in the table but are excluded from real-price comparisons.

Limits

        
    
            
  • CPI-U is general inflation. It answers "how much of my overall spending power did this phone take?", not "how did phone prices change for the same quality?" BLS publishes a quality-adjusted telephone-hardware index for that second question, and it falls steeply.
    • 
            
  • Base model only. Average selling prices are higher because many buyers choose Pro models and more storage.
    • 
            
  • Launch list price, not what people paid. Trade-ins, carrier promotions and installment plans aren't captured.
    • 
            
  • U.S. only, and a curated list. These are notable phones I chose, not a random sample. Budget phones are absent, so this says nothing about the cheapest way to own a phone.
    • 
            
  • Some historic prices are approximate. They're marked "medium" confidence with the reported range in the note.
